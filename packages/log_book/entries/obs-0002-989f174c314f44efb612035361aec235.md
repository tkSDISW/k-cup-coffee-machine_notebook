## Reference Specifications (preliminary) and a model-mismatch issue

Per the "capture reference resources before modeling" practice. This is the starting list; items marked OPEN still need to be captured before the related phase begins.

**Issue: the selected sources describe three different Keurig models.** The model needs one defined basis, or we must explicitly treat the sources as a representative product family.

| Source | Exact model covered | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| SmartKeurig repo (shildenbrand, GitHub) | **Keurig K40** | control board interfaces, sensors, I/O (LA / PA) |
| Ohio State Keurig project, Phase 1 | **Keurig B130** | component teardown, materials, K-Cup holder (PA) |
| iFixit device page | **Keurig K-Duo** (dual-function: K-Cup pods and carafe) | published specs (SA requirements, PA properties) |

The K-Duo also brews ground coffee into a carafe, which a K-Cup-only model would exclude. Its specs are therefore context, not a direct basis, unless we decide otherwise.

**Specs captured so far**

*Keurig K40* (source: SmartKeurig README, https://github.com/shildenbrand/SmartKeurig)
- Inputs observed on the user interface board: water-level pressure sensor, power button, brew size buttons, normally-closed limit switch on the open/close lever (detects K-Cup placement). A temperature sensor is inferred, because the machine knows when the water is hot, but it was not visible.
- Outputs: five status LEDs; power to a pump motor.
- Internal microcontroller was not accessed, and the board connects to it by a 16-pin cable. Controller behavior is therefore **inferred**, not documented.

*Keurig B130* (source: Ohio State teardown, https://u.osu.edu/keurigproject/phase-1-understanding-product-and-process/initial-research-manufacturing-of-a-pod-coffee-maker)
- Lever handle opens and closes the lid and punctures the K-Cup; the pod is pierced by metal needles at top and bottom.
- Removable K-Cup holder assembly, user-cleaned, made of polypropylene.
- Brewing assembly built from three components, all PC/GF, connected by a guide rail.
- Handle is painted ABS, fixed with two screws and a clip. The button stack is a clear polycarbonate button, spring, rubber gasket and PCBA.

*Keurig K-Duo* (source: iFixit, https://www.ifixit.com/Device/Keurig_K-Duo)
- 1470 W, 120 VAC, 50/60 Hz; 60 oz water reservoir; brew temperature 170-190 °F; single-cup sizes 6, 8, 10 and 12 oz; dimensions 12.92 x 10.94 x 12.76 in (H x W x D); brewer weight 9.71 lb.

**OPEN items**
1. **Choose the basis model** (suggested: **K40**, since the SmartKeurig sources describe its behavior and interfaces, with the B130 teardown as supporting physical detail). Record the decision.
2. **Locate the user manual** for the chosen basis model (OA/SA source) and log its exact title, revision and URL.
3. **Obtain K40 nameplate specs** (power, voltage, reservoir volume): none captured yet.
4. **Check licenses** of each source before copying any text or figures into the model.
5. Treat all numbers above as **unverified secondary-source values** until confirmed against the manufacturer's documentation.

## Update: the two selected sources, read in full

This supersedes the earlier verification note: the iFixit and Ohio State pages have now both been read in full, not just seen as excerpts.

### iFixit: Keurig K-Duo (2019), [device page](https://www.ifixit.com/Device/Keurig_K-Duo)
- **It is a hybrid brewer:** K-Cup pod brewing and a drip carafe from one 60 oz reservoir; 12-cup glass carafe with hot plate, programmable auto-brew, pause and pour.
- **Specs confirmed on the page:** 1470 W, 120 VAC 50/60 Hz; brew temperature 170-190 °F; single cups of 6, 8, 10 and 12 oz; 12.92 x 10.94 x 12.76 in; brewer 9.71 lb. Exterior materials are PP, PC and ABS; the carafe is stainless steel and PP.
- **User manual found (the OA/SA source we wanted):** [Keurig K-Duo Essentials Use and Care Guide (PDF)](https://www.ifixit.com/Document/2TnuJEnph6X2TRdB/Keurig-K-Duo-Essentials-Use-and-Care-Guide.pdf). Also linked: [K-Duo Plus FAQ (PDF)](https://www.ifixit.com/Document/OO5TSCrJZTYZJAGa/Keurig-K-Duo-Plus-FAQ.pdf) and a [Keurig coffee maker comparison chart (PDF)](https://www.ifixit.com/Document/VwDDLIM4Rf6Hl5uR/Keurig-Coffee-Maker-Comparison-Chart.pdf). **Not yet read.**
- **Component-level guides, useful for LA/PA structure:** replacement guides for the [air pump](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Keurig+K-Duo+Air+Pump+Replacement/179988), [hot plate](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Keurig+K-Duo+Hot+Plate+Replacement/179985), [motherboard](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Keurig+K-Duo+Motherboard+Replacement/179651), [pod holder](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Keurig+K-Duo+Pod+Holder+Replacement/182941), [rear plastic panels](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Keurig+K-Duo+Rear+Plastic+Panels+Replacement/179472) and [water pump](https://www.ifixit.com/Guide/Keurig+K-Duo+Water+Pump+Replacement/179986). **Not yet read.**
- **Troubleshooting pages** (does not turn on, not brewing a full pot, does not reach target temperature) could seed failure-mode thinking. Page content is user-contributed (student team), so it is secondary.

### Ohio State teardown: Keurig B130, [page](https://u.osu.edu/keurigproject/phase-1-understanding-product-and-process/initial-research-manufacturing-of-a-pod-coffee-maker)
- **Provenance:** the B130 teardown section is a student summary of a Fictiv teardown, [Keurig B130 Coffee Brewing System Teardown](https://www.fictiv.com/teardowns/keurig-b130-coffee-brewing-system-teardown). **Go to the original for authoritative detail.** Not yet read.
- **User interface (B130):** push-button switches with LEDs: green "Place Cup", red "Heating", cyan "Brew", and a separate green "Power" button; a "Press to Open" button releases the lid.
- **Sensing and control:** a thermocouple on the PCBA against the base plate (thermal paste for accuracy); a thermostat tracking tank temperature; a switch detecting whether the lid is closed; a sensor on the water tank lid; a level reference used to meter water to the cup.
- **Hydraulics and actuators:** air pumps driven by voltage signals push air into the top of the water heater; hydraulic parts include a POM component holding a steel "water only" plate and rubber seals above and below it.
- **Pod interface:** the handle opens and closes the lid and drives the metal needles that pierce the K-Cup top and bottom; the pod holder is removable for cleaning.
- **Pod context (OA):** each pod holds 13-18 g of ground coffee; cup, filter and foil lid with nitrogen flush; outer ridges keep the filter off the walls.
- **Materials summary:** ABS (most used), PP (less critical parts), POM and PC/GF (mechanically essential parts). Electronics are kept simple; elastomer gaskets waterproof them.
- **Operational context, new lead for OA/SA:** the same site has a Phase 2 on stakeholders, context and constraints: [Interview of a Keurig User](https://u.osu.edu/keurigproject/phase-2-understanding-stakeholders-context-and-constraints/interview-of-a-keurig-user/), [Cognitive Walkthrough of a Keurig](https://u.osu.edu/keurigproject/phase-2-understanding-stakeholders-context-and-constraints/cognitive-walkthrough-of-a-keurig/) and [Reflection and Constraints](https://u.osu.edu/keurigproject/phase-2-understanding-stakeholders-context-and-constraints/reflection-and-constraints/), plus [Basics of Pod Coffee Makers](https://u.osu.edu/keurigproject/phase-1-understanding-product-and-process/initial-research/). **Not yet read.**
- **Licensing:** student course site; check terms before reusing any text or figures.
