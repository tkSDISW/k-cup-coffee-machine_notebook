## behavior_config: K Cup Coffee Maker Notebook

**Style and rigor**
- Entries read as short, dense engineering notes with the rationale included. Use proper ARCADIA terms (operational entity, operational activity, system function, logical component, physical node, and so on).
- Moderate rigor: state assumptions and open questions explicitly; do not over-polish.
- Each modeling decision names the ARCADIA phase it belongs to and cites its source, with a link.
- Entries about model changes reference the affected element names or UUIDs.
- Mark each statement as sourced (with citation), inferred (with the reasoning), or assumed (logged as an assumption).

**Neutral and non-company-specific (this notebook is meant to be shared with the product's maker and with makers of future products)**
- Keep the notebook and model **not tied to any company**. Use generic terms in model element names and prose (for example "single-serve pod brewer", "pod", "pod holder") rather than brand-specific terminology.
- Name a specific company or product **only where needed to cite a source or record a reference specification** (for example the model number in a reference-specs entry). Do not build brand names into element names or requirements.
- **Make no judgments about the design.** Describe what the design does and how it is built; do not evaluate it. Avoid evaluative words and implied criticism (for example "poor", "flawed", "weak", "cheap", "unnecessary", "should have").
- Report observations as **neutral facts attributed to their source**. Where a source expresses an opinion, record that the source said it, or leave it out; do not adopt it.
- Where a design choice is noted, record the **operational need it serves or the constraint it responds to**, not whether it was the right choice. Alternatives may be recorded as options, never as corrections.
- Keep the tone **respectful and appreciative of the engineering**. Success factors (such as convenience and lack of mess) are described as outcomes that the operational analysis explains.
- Findings that point toward future products are phrased as **opportunities or open questions**, not as shortcomings of current products.

**Before sharing:** review entries for evaluative wording, verify sources and licenses, and remove anything that reads as criticism of a company, product or person.
