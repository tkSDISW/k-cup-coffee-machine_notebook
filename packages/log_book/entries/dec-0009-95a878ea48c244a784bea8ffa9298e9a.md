## Decision: tuning choices for the base and goal requirements

**Decided by the engineer**, answering the tuning questions in NOTE-0003 and NOTE-0004.

1. **Serving temperature is from the user's perspective.** The requirement is the temperature the drinker experiences at the point of delivery. Brew water temperature is not a user requirement; it stays a reference data point and becomes a derived value in System Analysis and later phases.
2. **Strength baseline: both.** Strength is defined relative to **(a)** a published reference (the brewing control chart, as a candidate) **and (b)** a **project-defined named baseline** to be set by us. Each variety and strength setting is expressed against both.
3. **Waste baseline: "coffee in, coffee out".** The minimum possible waste is the used coffee remains themselves; **everything else, in particular packaging, counts as waste.** As understood: the waste goals measure what is discarded beyond the used coffee remains, and a design is compared against that minimum rather than against another product. *Interpretation to confirm with the engineer.*
4. **Scope of the problem space: use and packaging.** The sustainability goals cover the **use phase and the packaging** of coffee supplies. The coffee supply chain (growing, roasting, shipping) is **out of scope**. As understood, manufacturing of the equipment itself is also outside the boundary for now. *To confirm with the engineer.*

**Consequences**
- UR-B-02 stays as drafted (drinker's perspective); brew water temperature stays as a reference in NOTE-0003.
- UR-B-03 now names two baselines, with the project baseline still to be defined.
- UR-G-01, UR-G-02 and UR-G-04 are reworded around "coffee in, coffee out": waste beyond the used coffee remains, and packaging mass relative to the coffee it carries.
- UR-G-08 uses a use-and-packaging boundary.
- The comparison in DEC-0005 for convenience and mess indicators is not changed by this decision.

**Open points**
- Define the project-defined strength baseline (item 2b).
- Confirm items 3 and 4 as worded.
- Decide whether the equipment's own end-of-life belongs in the boundary, since it is a use-adjacent question (see UR-G-09).
