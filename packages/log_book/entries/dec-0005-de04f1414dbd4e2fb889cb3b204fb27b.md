## Decision: Operational Analysis is the centerpiece, organized around convenience and lack of mess

**Decided by the engineer.** The model starts with Operational Analysis (OA), and OA is treated as the main deliverable, not a preamble.

**Thesis (engineer's insight, adopted as the organizing idea):** the pod system succeeded mainly because of **convenience** and **lack of mess**. Those are operational outcomes, so OA is where the product's success is explained, and it sets the bar that any next-generation product must meet or beat.

**How this shapes the work**
- **OA captures the operational need, not the machine.** It describes what people and organizations do to get a cup of coffee, and where effort and mess occur, independent of any system boundary.
- **Candidate OA content (proposal, not yet modeled):**
  - Operational entities and actors: the person making coffee, the household or office, the pod producer, retail and supply, waste handling.
  - Operational activities of the **incumbent way** of making a cup (for example measuring, filtering, brewing, disposing of grounds, cleaning up) compared with the **pod way**, to show which activities the pod removes, shortens, or moves upstream to the pod producer.
  - Operational capabilities and scenarios built around "get a single cup quickly, with minimal cleanup".
- **Convenience and mess become traceable value drivers.** Each should be stated as an operational need in OA, carried into SA as requirements, and traced down to the LA and PA choices that deliver it (for example a sealed single-serve pod, a puncturing holder, a drip tray). Any such trace must cite its source or be logged as an assumption.
- **Proposed operational benchmark (not yet defined):** express convenience and mess as countable indicators (for example user steps, time to cup, cleanup actions, mess events) for the baseline and the pod, so a next-generation concept can be judged against the bar this model sets.

**Goal:** the finished model should **surpass the Gaphor coffee machine tutorial**, which is the baseline for what people are likely to find today (see the benchmark observation).

**Open points:** which incumbent processes to include as the baseline; how to phrase the benchmark indicators; whether some operational needs are supported by our sources (K-Duo manual, Ohio State pages) or must be logged as assumptions.
