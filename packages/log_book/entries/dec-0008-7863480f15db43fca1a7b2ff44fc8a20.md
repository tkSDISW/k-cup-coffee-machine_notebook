## Decision: OA capabilities stay solution-agnostic; the pod and other design terms enter in later phases

**Decided by the engineer**, while tuning the first draft of operational capabilities (NOTE-0002).

**Principle.** Highest-level OA capabilities state the operational need and do not refer to the single-serve pod, which is a design implementation. The same goes for other implementation terms (pod holder, needles, drip tray, reservoir, pump, heater and similar). They are introduced in System Analysis, Logical Architecture and Physical Architecture, where the design is described.

**Why this matters for the project**
- It keeps OA a statement of **why** (convenience and lack of mess, DEC-0005) so it can be reused to judge any design, including next-generation concepts.
- It lets the model show **how a design realizes the need**: the pod's value appears as the way later phases deliver the OA needs, not as something assumed at the start.
- It keeps the notebook neutral about the design (behavior_config).

**How it is applied**
- Capability names use generic words such as "coffee-making equipment", "ready-to-use coffee supplies" and "used coffee remains and packaging".
- Evidence from the manual that describes the implementation stays in the notebook as evidence and is tagged for the phase where it will be used.
- The scope decision in DEC-0004 (pod-brewing mode of the K-Duo) is unchanged: it defines the system modeled from SA onward, not the OA wording.

**Open point:** the qualifier "single serving" in capability 1 may itself be a need (an amount for one person) or an implementation choice (versus a carafe). Left as "a serving" in the revised draft and flagged for tuning.
