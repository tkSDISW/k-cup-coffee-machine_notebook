## Reference point: what the Gaphor coffee machine tutorial covers

**Source:** [Gaphor Tutorial: Coffee Machine](https://docs.gaphor.org/en/latest/coffee_machine.html) (landing page read in full; the sub-pages listed below were **not** read yet). Notation is SysML in the Gaphor tool.

**Subject:** a custom **espresso machine for a cat-run café**, told as a playful story. It is a teaching example, not a product model, and is a good indication of what people are likely to find today when they look for an open coffee machine model.

**Structure, from the page**
- Three abstraction levels: **Concept** (the problem being solved), **Logical** (a technology-agnostic solution), **Technology** (the detailed solution). A fourth, Implementation, is explicitly not modeled.
- Four SysML pillars: behavior, structure, requirements, parametric. The tutorial says it focuses on the first three and leaves out parametrics.
- The tutorial's contents list:
  - **Concept level:** [use case diagram](https://docs.gaphor.org/en/latest/coffee_machine_concept.html#use-case-diagram), domain diagram, context diagram, concept requirements.
  - **Logical level:** [functional boundary behavior](https://docs.gaphor.org/en/latest/coffee_machine_logical.html#functional-boundary-behavior), logical state machine, logical structure, logical boundary, logical requirements.
  - A [summary page](https://docs.gaphor.org/en/latest/coffee_machine_wrapup.html).
- The example model ships in Gaphor's `examples` folder as `coffee-machine`.

**Opportunities to extend this reference point** (hypotheses from the contents list only; to be confirmed by reading the concept, logical and summary pages)
1. **Operational grounding.** The concept level is organized around use cases and context. An Operational Analysis of what people do, and where effort and mess occur, would add the "why" behind those use cases. This is the centerpiece of our model (DEC-0005).
2. **Real product basis.** The tutorial's machine is fictional by design, so its requirements are not tied to a specific product. Ours would trace to a real product (K-Duo, pod-brewing mode) with cited specs and a teardown.
3. **Reaching the physical level.** The contents list shows concept and logical walkthroughs; a physical (technology) walkthrough is not listed. Our plan is to continue through Physical Architecture with teardown-grounded components.
4. **Explicit traceability.** Needs, requirements, functions and components linked end to end, with exchanges named by type (PMEI practice).
5. **Open process record.** Sources, assumptions and decisions captured in this notebook.

**Note:** the tutorial is built to teach SysML, so these are differences of purpose and depth, not a comparison of quality or of notations.
