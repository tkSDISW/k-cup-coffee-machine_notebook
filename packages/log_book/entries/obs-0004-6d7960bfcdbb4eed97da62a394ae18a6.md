## Benchmark: what the Gaphor coffee machine tutorial covers (the bar to surpass)

**Source:** [Gaphor Tutorial: Coffee Machine](https://docs.gaphor.org/en/latest/coffee_machine.html) (landing page read in full; the sub-pages listed below were **not** read yet). Notation is SysML in the Gaphor tool.

**Subject:** a custom **espresso machine for a cat-run café**, told as a playful story. It is a teaching example, not a product model.

**Structure, from the page**
- Three abstraction levels: **Concept** (the problem being solved), **Logical** (a technology-agnostic solution), **Technology** (the detailed solution). A fourth, Implementation, is explicitly not modeled.
- Four SysML pillars: behavior, structure, requirements, parametric. The tutorial says it focuses on the first three and skips parametrics.
- The tutorial's contents list:
  - **Concept level:** [use case diagram](https://docs.gaphor.org/en/latest/coffee_machine_concept.html#use-case-diagram), domain diagram, context diagram, concept requirements.
  - **Logical level:** [functional boundary behavior](https://docs.gaphor.org/en/latest/coffee_machine_logical.html#functional-boundary-behavior), logical state machine, logical structure, logical boundary, logical requirements.
  - A [summary page](https://docs.gaphor.org/en/latest/coffee_machine_wrapup.html).
- The example model ships in Gaphor's `examples` folder as `coffee-machine`.

**Where we can plausibly surpass it** (hypotheses from the contents list only; to be confirmed by reading the concept, logical and summary pages)
1. **Operational grounding.** The concept level starts from use cases and context, not from an operational analysis of what people do and where effort and mess occur. Our OA centerpiece (DEC-0005) goes deeper on the "why".
2. **Real product basis.** The Gaphor machine is fictional, so its requirements cannot be checked against a real product. Ours traces to a real model (K-Duo) with cited specs and a teardown.
3. **Reaching the physical level.** The contents list shows concept and logical walkthroughs but **no physical (technology) walkthrough**. We plan to go through PA with teardown-grounded components.
4. **Explicit traceability.** Needs, requirements, functions and components linked end to end, with exchanges named by type (PMEI practice).
5. **Open process record.** Every source, assumption and decision in this notebook, which a tutorial does not provide.

**Caveat:** this is a tutorial built to teach SysML, so "surpassing" it means depth, realism and traceability, not a fairer comparison of notations.
