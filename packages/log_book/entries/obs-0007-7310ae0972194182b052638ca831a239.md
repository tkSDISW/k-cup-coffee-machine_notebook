## Reference: "Augmenting requirements with models" (Bonnet, Voirin, Navas, INCOSE IS 2019)

Reference capture for the requirements work in NOTE-0003 and NOTE-0004. The summary below is paraphrased; the paper is copyrighted by its author with permission granted to INCOSE, so check terms before reusing text or figures.

**Citation:** Stéphane Bonnet, Jean-Luc Voirin and Juan Navas (Thales), *Augmenting requirements with models to improve the articulation between system engineering levels and optimize V&V practices*, INCOSE International Symposium 2019. The paper carries the INCOSE Best MBSE Paper Award mark on the copy hosted by the Capella site, and a Capella forum reply refers to it as the 2019 best MBSE paper.

**Thesis, in short.** Requirements remain the backbone of systems engineering even with MBSE, but they can be textual statements **or model elements**. The two **complete each other** and together form the technical contract between engineering levels, with stronger verification and validation as a consequence. (This is the "models as an extension of requirements" idea.)

**Ideas we will use**
1. **A three-legged stool:** textual requirements, a **need model** (Operational and System Need Analysis, which formalize and consolidate the customer and system requirements), and a **solution model** (Logical and Physical Architecture, which validate feasibility and elicit or justify new requirements).
2. **Model requirements:** functional requirements can be represented by Capabilities, Functions, Functional Exchanges, Functional Chains, Scenarios, Modes and States. Interface requirements by Functional and Component Exchanges and data structures (data types, classes, physical quantities). Some non-functional requirements by properties on model elements (for example a latency constraint on a Functional Chain).
3. **What stays textual:** the paper says environmental constraints, applicable standards and norms, and required maintenance periods are hard to express as model elements, and that some model elements are too complex or too fine-grained to be good requirements. So models do not replace textual requirements.
4. **Linking rules:** model requirements that formalize a textual one are linked to it by traceability or justification links; textual requirements about a model element are linked by satisfaction links; requirements that apply to part of the system link to the matching components.
5. **Customer requirements that are really about the solution** (for example an imposed technology) should attach directly to Logical or Physical Architecture elements, not be forced into the need models.
6. **Textual requirement quality tasks:** reformulate into clear, unambiguous statements (for example, from "the user shall be able to..." to "upon a given user action, the system shall..."), identify a verification method (inspection, analysis, demonstration or test), and ensure traceability.
7. **Keep need-originated and solution requirements in separate modules,** according to configuration-management scopes, so lifecycles stay independent.
8. **V&V through the model:** test campaigns are defined with Capabilities, Scenarios and Functional Chains (nominal and degraded), not only requirement sets. A model requirement is verified when its related tests pass; a textual requirement is verified when its related model requirements are verified.
9. **Increments (Requested and Developed Versions)** let the model show which capabilities are available when components are late. Likely illustrative only for this single-level educational project.

**How it relates to this project**
- Our OA centerpiece (DEC-0005) matches the paper's need-model step, where stakeholders and objectives are modeled with operational activities and processes and traced into System Analysis.
- Our rule that OA stays solution-agnostic (DEC-0008) is consistent with item 5.
- The "Capability" column in NOTE-0003 and NOTE-0004 is the link between textual requirements and operational capabilities that the paper recommends.
- **Not covered by the paper:** our split into base requirements and goal (reach) requirements. That structure is our own.
- **Context difference:** the paper is written for customer and supplier levels in a large organization. Our project is a single-level educational model, so subcontracting and subsystem-transition material applies as illustration only.
